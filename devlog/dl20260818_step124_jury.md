**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Purpose:** Three-AI read-only investigation (Claude Fable + Codex + Grok) into why
tapestack3d timestep 124 burned a 38-minute rejected attempt on the pre-branch binary
(devel 27752f83 + uncommitted) while the `bitset_and_spmatrix_fix` binary (a8331d05)

## Verdict (three-voice consensus, confidence medium-high)

**H3 — restart ≠ continuation, plus fresh solver re-initialization — is the
best-supported explanation for the split. The sparse-matrix commits are real
correctness fixes, but "they made 124 converge" is unproven.**

The comparison was never a clean A/B:

- The **old** process restored the t = 1800 dump and reached 124 **live**
  (evolving through steps 119–123; STRUMPACK matching computed at step 119's
  Δt = 50 ms system, never recomputed).
- The **new** process warm-restarted from the dump the old run itself wrote at
  step 123 / t = 1850, re-initializing STRUMPACK (fresh matching) at 124.
- Magnetic Picard-1 matched to six printed digits (same restored field —
  trivially, since the dump came from the old run's own accepted step 123),
  but **thermal Picard-1 split 0.00 dB vs −82.99 dB**: the solver states were
  not equivalent. Under full coupling (T → jc, n, ρ_Cu) that shifts magnetic
  Picard-2 (−82.40 vs −71.47 dB), and from a −71 dB nearly-converged Picard
  iterate the old run took the documented Newton-3 kick (−71 → −61 dB) and
  ω-crawled to the 30-iteration cap. The new run's −82 dB Picard-2 took a
  contractive Newton step and finished.

Honest framing of the original question: the old binary did **not** terminally
fail 124. It rejected once, accepted the Δt = 5.28 ms retry, then rejected 125
once as well — the same ω-crawl class it had already shown twice at step 119.
A hard-step / restart-state pathology the old controller already owned.

## Retractions (Claude, on vendor refutation)

1. "H3 is dead because Picard-1 is bit-identical" — retracted. Picard-1 is a
   pre-update entry residual; it proves the restored field matched, not the
   solver state (Grok R4; thermal V2 table).
2. The STRUMPACK `GMRES it. 0` gap (6892 vs 0.408) as corruption evidence —
   withdrawn. That residual lives on the matched/scaled system; the old
   process's matching was stale from step 119, the new one fresh. Decisive
   counter-evidence: the old binary accepted steps 120–123 at −111…−116 dB,
   which a corrupted assembly cannot do.
3. Earlier recommendation to restart the campaign from `memdump_1800.hdf5` —
   withdrawn. The t = 1825 → 1850 segment was evolved at accepted
   −111…−116 dB; no corruption demonstrated or expected on this deck.

## The commits themselves (real, latent here)

- **H1, `SpMatrix::allocate_values` sentinel:** the old `malloc(nnz)` plus
  index()-miss return of `nnz` was a genuine release-mode one-past-the-end
  write (ASan-confirmed; encoded in `test_SpMatrix.cpp:1754-1780`). Blast
  radius on past tapestack3d STRUMPACK results: **low** — a miss loses the
  same contribution on either binary, the overflow slot sat in the matrix's
  own mmap padding at this nnz, and the solver never reads slot nnz. The fix
  matters for sanitizer/guard-page builds and any future allocation geometry.
- **H2, base-flip of shared index arrays:** the old multiply physically
  rebased parent/child-shared index arrays around the Fortran kernel. Restore
  was correct; the dangerous consumer is MUMPS-class (Fortran-base) decks —
  **medium** risk there until assembly-after-Cpp-restore is proven. STRUMPACK
  and PETSc force C-indexing, so low on this deck. The separate stale-child
  `mIndexFunction` dispatcher bug is structurally eliminated on the branch
  (the symbol no longer exists; `matvec_csr`/`matvec_csc` take the base as an
  argument).
- **H4 (CSC atomic → reduction) and the DynamicBitset hardening:** correctness
  and reproducibility improvements; neither can produce an 11 dB Picard split
  on the CSR residual path.
