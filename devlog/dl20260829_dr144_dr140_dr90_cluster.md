# DR-144 / DR-140 / DR-90: The MUMPS-Lifecycle and Conditioning Cluster

**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Purpose:** Scope the three rows Christian identified as a cluster, re-verify each against the
working tree rather than against the register text that filed it, and open the DR-144 fix round.
**Module:** `src/sparse`, `src/fem/kernel`

## What tied the three together

DR-140 (a symmetric `SymmetryMode` makes MUMPS factorize a different matrix) and DR-144 (seven
instance-lifecycle accounting gaps) are both debt in the machinery the conditioning diagnostic runs
on. DR-90 (the omega geometric-recovery rule, RUN-BLOCKED) is in the cluster because the
conditioning diagnostic is the *instrument* its open question needs: whether the residual stall band
it describes is conditioning noise or a linear-solver tolerance.

That reading held, but the DR-90 half resolved differently than expected. Details below.

## DR-90: the gate is inapplicable, and the conditioning half was already on disk

The row is `[RUN-BLOCKED]` because its gate names a `t = 2300 ms` memdump that was deleted. Two
things turned out to be true that the row does not know:

**The rel-tol A/B cannot be run against this deck at all.** The magnetic field now uses
`library : mumps` (`cmake-build-debug/tapestack3d/input.conf:18`), a direct solver. The deck's
`relative tolerance : 1e-10` carries the annotation `// for PETSC` and never reaches MUMPS.
Christian's suspicion — a magnetic relative tolerance sitting three decades above the nonlinear
target — was framed against an iterative magnetic solver this deck no longer uses. Recovering the
dump would not have made the gate runnable.

**The conditioning half is already answered by surviving logs.** `cmake-build-debug/tapestack3d/out.txt`,
from the 2026-08-29 20:20-20:28 PDT run (warm restart at t = 4450 ms, steps 216-229), reports a
magnetic conditioning number every timestep from two independent estimators:

| estimator | range over steps 216-229 |
|---|---|
| `\|lambda\|max/\|lambda\|min, Magnetic` (eigen spectral ratio) | 6.03e9 to 7.4e10 |
| `MUMPS Arioli-Demmel-Duff COND1` | 4.25e6 to 3.43e8 |

The implied eps*kappa noise floor is -48 dB from the spectral ratio and -90 dB from COND1. Magnetic
residuals achieved in the same run: -70 to -96 dB. The residuals therefore sit **at or below** the
worst-case bound. That does not exclude conditioning noise, but it does not force it either:
eps*kappa is a loose upper bound and beating it proves only that it is loose. Candidate (a) is not
settled. Confidence: medium.

**And the stall class does not reproduce.** Both rejections in that run are thermal-limited, not
magnetic:

- Step 224: the magnetic residual is flat at -70.1 dB while the thermal field grinds -38 -> -52 dB
  and exhausts its iteration budget. No magnetic sawtooth.
- Step 229: a thermal divergence to +33 dB. A different class entirely.

The reason is visible in the deck: magnetic `tolerance : 1e-7`, four decades looser than the ~1e-11
the original DR-90 trace was chasing. The -85...-108 dB band the row is about now lies *below* the
convergence target instead of stalling against it.

**By-catch worth more than the row it came from:** at step 224 the magnetic field clears its
tolerance by **0.1 dB** (-70.1 dB achieved against a -70 dB target) and stops iterating, after which
the coupled step dies on the partner field. A field that clears its tolerance by a tenth of a dB is
not converged in any robust sense. This is a live observation on today's binary, not a historical
trace.

Proposed re-specified gate, recorded in the row for Christian's ruling: drop the rel-tol A/B as
inapplicable, and run the current MUMPS-magnetic deck at the DR-90-era magnetic tolerance (1e-11),
reading the per-step COND1 against the achieved floor. Executable today; no recovered dump needed.

DR-90 is the fourth register row lost to evidence decay and **the first recovered from it** — only
because the run logs happened to survive. That is exactly the habit `run_gate_batches.md` §6 asks
for.

## DR-144: smaller than its row, and the deferral reason was wrong

All seven gaps re-verified open against the working tree, which has moved since filing (DR-143's
first-free scan and the soft-fail create both landed). The round's main finding:

**Gaps 3 and 4 collapse into one line, and the register's reason for deferring them is refuted.**
The row says they "need their own plan round because the `mInitialized` semantics carry the
frozen-factorization and soft-fail machinery." Measured: `MUMPS::mInitialized`
(`cl_SolverMUMPS.hpp:75`) is written at three solve sites (`:609`, `:643`, `:783`) and **read at
exactly one**, `MUMPS::free():284`. It touches neither `mFrozen` nor `soft_fail()` — the frozen
scope is carried by `mFrozen.mArmed` and `mMatrix`, and the soft-fail retry keys on the *separate*
`Wrapper::mIsInitialized` through `is_initialized()`. Note the two flags are different members; that
is the trap the register fell into.

A refinement found while testing the plan's own falsification condition: **G3 as filed is not
reachable through the `Solver` API at all.** `Solver::initialize` is never called by a client — it
is lazy, invoked from inside `Solver::solve` (`cl_Solver.cpp:158-161`, `:193-196`). The gap is
reachable only through the public `wrapper()` back door (`cl_Solver.hpp:187`), which is exactly what
the shift-invert consumer uses. G4 remains reachable on its own terms. This narrows G3's severity
without removing it, and it is what makes the R9 unit gate constructible.

Plan: `todo/dr144_mumps_lifecycle_plan.md`, registered in `todo/README.md`, scope approved by
Christian ("DR-144 in full, one round").

### The jury round: gaps confirmed, steps rejected

Codex returned **revise before implementation**, Grok **approve with the required corrections**.
Both confirmed G1-G7 and F1 against the tree. Both rejected the *step shapes*. The useful lesson is
that the round found almost nothing wrong with the diagnosis and almost everything wrong with the
prescriptions — which is the opposite of the failure mode a plan audit is usually guarding against.

**The seven gaps are now nine.** Both new ones are Grok's, and both verified here before acceptance:

- **G8** — a second `MUMPS::initialize()` creates a slot and overwrites `mSolverID`, losing the
  first ID, and only *then* calls `Wrapper::initialize()`, which throws on its already-initialized
  check (`cl_SolverWrapper.cpp:64-66`). Both slots leak and the first becomes unreachable.
- **G9** — `mumpstools_create_solver` passes `aInfo` as `MPI_BARRIER`'s status argument (`:103`).
  `aInfo` is `integer( int_t )` (`:64`), 64-bit under `BELFEM_INT64`, where a default `integer` is
  required. `mumpstools_free_solver` gets this right with its own `integer :: ierr` (`:127`). The
  value is clobbered at `:107` regardless, so the barrier status is discarded either way.

**Three of the fix shapes the register itself proposed were wrong:**

1. *"Bound-check the free"* — v1 put the check ahead of the `allocated` guard. `size()` on an
   unallocated allocatable is undefined, and the guard exists precisely because a full drain
   deallocates. Both auditors caught it independently, both HIGH.
2. *"Move the barrier ahead of the occupancy decision"* — this does **not** make exhaustion
   collective. Each rank still scans its own process-local table; on disagreement one returns while
   the other enters `DMUMPS`, which is itself collective on `MPI_COMM_WORLD`. The hang relocates
   from the explicit barrier into MUMPS. The honest step is a barrier on each *agreeing* arm, with
   rank consensus split out as its own open question (O4) rather than smuggled into a barrier move.
3. *"Or key the Fortran free on `mSolverID > 0`"* — the register offered this as an equivalent
   alternative. It is not. The ID is never cleared, so a freed-but-remembered ID names a slot the
   first-free scan has since handed to another wrapper: `~A` then issues `JOB = -2` against **B's
   live instance**, and `gOccupied` reads 1 so the shim does not skip it. That is a cross-wrapper
   destroy, not a benign double free. The fix is to zero the ID on release (new step R10), after
   which consolidating onto a single handle becomes safe — and is deliberately left to a follow-up.

Codex and Grok appeared to disagree on that third point (Codex recommended the single-handle
consolidation, Grok refuted it) but did not: Codex's own proposal included zeroing the ID. The split
was over whether the boolean then goes away, which is a scope question, not a correctness one.

Also corrected: R1's message would have relabelled pool exhaustion as a factorization failure —
`wrapper()->failed()` is raised by both — so it now names a linear-solver failure without pinning it
to factors, with an explicit `Infeasible` arm and a numeric default so the next enumerator does not
repeat G6. R9's "eighth cycle exhausts the pool" was off by one (eight empty slots, unpinned: cycle
**nine** is the first exhaustion). And `gNumSolvers` turns out to be write-only — four writes, zero
reads — so the rollback decrement is bookkeeping, not authority.

**Pre-registration scored:** P2 (the barrier move is not equivalent) hit, from both auditors, and
was the round's most valuable finding. P3 (an auditor prefers `mSolverID > 0`) hit. P5 (a gap I did
not file) hit twice. P1 (a live initialize-then-free path) was not found by either auditor, and I
had already narrowed it myself before the round returned — `Solver::initialize` is lazy inside
`Solver::solve`, so G3 is reachable only through the public `wrapper()` back door. Grok added the
case I missed: a first-solve `BELFEM_ERROR` throw fires *before* today's `mInitialized` write, so a
caught error after a successful create leaks too.

Five of the eleven steps (R3, R4, R5, R6, R11) have no reachable in-tree trigger and will land
**reviewed, not verified**. The plan says so explicitly rather than letting the shared `make check`
imply coverage it does not give.

Read-only was honoured: no auditor modified source. The only file that moved during the round was
`src/numerics/sources/cl_SourceFunction.hpp`, a co-running session's work, left untouched.

## Christian's ruling, and what landed

Both asks approved in one word: code DR-144, and strike DR-90.

### DR-90 struck

Archived to `debt_register_closed.md`. Not because the question was answered — the eps*kappa bound
is loose enough to neither force nor exclude conditioning noise — but because the row could no longer
be settled on this deck by any means: the fix it describes landed in August, its gate is
inapplicable to a direct solver, and the stall class does not reproduce. The re-specified gate is

The 0.1 dB convergence margin observed at step 224 does **not** die with the row. It has nothing to
do with omega, and is flagged in the strike note as live.

### DR-144 implemented — nine gaps, nine steps

All of G1-G9 landed in one pass, with every correction the jury round required:

| step | gap | what changed |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | G6 | `SolverFailed` **and** an explicit `Infeasible` arm, plus a default that prints the unmatched enumerator instead of reading as a diagnosis. Worded as a linear-solver failure, not a factorization failure, because `wrapper()->failed()` is also raised by an exhausted create |
| R2 | G3+G4 | `mInitialized = true` in `initialize()`, after `Wrapper::initialize()`; the three solve-site writes and the comments that justified them deleted. The member now has one write, one read, one clear |
| R3 | G1 | `JOB = -1` rollback: clear occupancy, decrement, `nullify` the slot's user pointers, return id -1. Makes the subroutine's own header comment true |
| R4 | G5+G9 | barrier kept **next to** `DMUMPS` where it belongs, a matching barrier added on the exhaustion arm, and both given a proper default-kind `ierr` |
| R5 | G2 | create gate is `mSolverID <= 0 \|\| tInfo < 0`, with `tInfo` carried into `mInfo(0)` |
| R6 | G7 | free bound check, ordered `allocated` → range → index |
| R10 | O2/O3 | `mSolverID` zeroed in `free()` and before every create — the step that closes the cross-wrapper destroy |
| R11 | G8 | always-active double-init guard at the top of `initialize()` |
| R7 | by-catch | the two stale `get_cond*` strings |

**O1 resolved to (b), delete.** The plan left the choice between a loud guard and deletion open.
Deletion won on the ground that `mumpstools.f90` has no error-reporting mechanism at all — no
`error stop`, no `write(stderr)` anywhere in the file — so guarding would have meant importing a new
failure idiom into a shim purely to protect an API with zero callers in `src/` or `tests/`.
`gMaxNumSolvers` is now genuinely fixed at 8 and says so at its declaration. This was the one
judgment call made inside approved scope, and it is trivially reversible.

**What was actually run:** `g++ -std=gnu++17 -fsyntax-only` clean on all three C++ files and the
test against the tree's Armadillo config, and `gfortran -fsyntax-only` clean on the shim — the
latter confirmed to be a real check by a deliberate negative control, because a syntax check that
silently fails to find `dmumps_struc.h` would also report success. **No test was executed.** R8
(`make check`) is owed and is Christian's to run; it is the same build that discharges DR-140.
Five of the steps (R3, R4, R5, R6, R11) have no reachable in-tree trigger and land **reviewed, not
verified** regardless of what `make check` says.

### The code round found the thing the diff could not see

Codex: *revise before merge*. Grok: *ship the mechanics after fixing D1 and D2 — do not treat this
as a clean bill of health*. Both independently confirmed the lifecycle core. And both led with the
same finding, which was **not in the new code at all**.

A parallel session had swept this file from `InfoLevel::Detailed` to `Verbose` — sensible for the
per-step ARPACK telemetry — and the sweep also caught `report_unavailable`. `Detailed` is 3,
`Verbose` is 4, `message()` prints on `<=`, and both debug executables construct
`gLog( InfoLevel::Detailed )` (`belfem.cpp:38`). So R1's whole reason for existing — the sentence
that stops a solver failure being reported as a Krylov-budget refusal — **would have shipped inert**,
invisible in the one configuration where somebody is debugging a diagnostic that went quiet.

That is worth dwelling on. The pre-registration explicitly told the auditors the demotion sweep was
another session's work and not under review. Both of them looked at it anyway, and both were right
to. A fix can be correct in every line and still be defeated by an adjacent change nobody thought to
include in the diff.

Restored to `Detailed` for those two calls only; the eight telemetry demotions around them left
alone, which is exactly what both auditors recommended. Four smaller reporting defects fixed in the
same pass, all of them the *same class* as G6 — a message that names the wrong subsystem:

- `print_soft_fail` promised "timestep will be cut" on a failed **create**. True for the
  timestepping solve; false for the conditioning diagnostic, which arms soft-fail itself and
  continues the step. The `-1000` case now gets its own line.
- The `NotPositiveDefinite` string still said "no spectral fold can reach it". The fold was retired
  in favour of shift-invert, which *can* return a negative lambda_min.
- The hard-fail create path (soft-fail off) printed a bare `-1000`, because the decode lives in
  `print_soft_fail` and that branch never reaches it.
- The C++ comment asserted the create-fail branch "cannot desynchronize the ranks" — a stronger
  claim than the Fortran two files away is willing to make about the same situation.

And one test defect: `MUMPSPoolRecyclesFreedSlots` did not encode the cross-wrapper destroy its own
comments describe. If a churn `free()` had destroyed the pin's slot, first-free would have handed
that slot straight back and all twelve solves would still have passed. It now solves through the pin
after the churn.

**Two findings were folded, not fixed — after being mis-filed first.** `EigenValues::mK` can dangle
on a worker after a `set_equation()`, and the `mSolverID <= 0` solve bail is silent even with
soft-fail off. Both are real, both sit in *other sessions'* uncommitted work, and the rule is not to
edit over that.

I filed them as DR-149 and DR-150. Christian rejected both, and he was right twice over:

- **The extend-don't-branch policy** was set the same day, by another session, in the register's own
  Maintenance section: *when a session's own gate or audit turns up a finding in the same contract or
  subsystem as the row being worked, widen that row's scope cell and put the work in its plan,
  rather than branching a fresh ID.* Both findings are the MUMPS wrapper lifecycle and the
  conditioning diagnostic's own reporter — the subsystem DR-144 already spans, since G6 lives in
  `cl_FEM_DofMgr_EigenValues.cpp` as well. They are now G10 and G11 on DR-144.
- **DR-149 was an ID collision**, which is the worse half. A parallel session had filed and withdrawn
  that exact ID hours earlier, and the register's rule is that IDs are never reused, withdrawn or
  not. My scan matched only lines beginning `| DR-` — so it saw rows, and a withdrawn ID lives in
  prose.

The uncomfortable part is that this session read `debt_register.md` five times and never read the
rules section the policy sits in. The same file. Layer 1 of `lessons_learned.md` was skipped at
session start for the same reason: the work looked like it started in the middle of a known
subsystem rather than at the top of a session.

**Pre-registration scored:** Q1 and Q2 not found — Grok explicitly verified the opposite of Q1. Q3,
the exhaustion-arm barrier I named as the step I was least sure of, was not raised as a defect;
both auditors attacked the *comment* that oversold it instead. Q5 — "something only a Fortran reader
catches" — missed in the direction I expected and hit in one I did not: the worst finding of the
round was in a file I had told the auditors was not mine.

DR-144's R8, DR-144's R9, and DR-140's owed rerun.

`SolverLifecycle.MUMPSFreesAnInstanceThatNeverSolved` genuinely executed.

regression tests had only ever been hand-linked, and the row itself said a scratch link cannot
verify build wiring. Struck on the reading that triangle extraction is a *feature* in its own todo
file rather than this row's residue — the row scoped it out in those words. If that reading is
wrong the row should be un-struck, not the feature quietly absorbed.

**DR-144 NOT struck.** G10 and G11 are live code residue, and the register's own rule is that a
residual living in another session's artifact is still a residual. The row keeps `[CODE]`, and keeps

means. Everything reachable on the row is fixed and gate-passed.

**And five steps are still not verified by this.** R3, R4, R5, R6 and R11 have no reachable in-tree
trigger — a failed `JOB = -1`, a bad free id, a double init, mixed rank occupancy. A green suite
